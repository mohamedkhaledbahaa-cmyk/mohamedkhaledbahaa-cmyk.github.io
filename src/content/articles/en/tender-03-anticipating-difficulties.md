**The cheapest risk to manage is the one you identify at tender stage.** After contract signature, the same issue costs many times more — in liquidated damages, rejected variations and disputes.

## A simple three-column plan

For every tender, we build a short plan with three questions:

→ **What is the difficulty?**
→ **What is the real cause?**
→ **What is our solution or measure?**

| Difficulty | Real cause | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| Long-lead transformers | Limited factory capacity | Early order + price lock with supplier |
| Unclear specification | Drawings and specs conflict | Formal RFI to the client |
| Demanding liquidated damages | Contract condition | Qualification in the offer or priced contingency |

The middle column is where most of the value is. "The supplier may be late" is not actionable; "the factory is running at full capacity" is — order early.

## Typical entries

Unclear specifications, long-lead equipment, demanding liquidated damages, bond requirements, site conditions, currency and price volatility.

## Nothing is left to "we'll see"

Each line ends up as one of three things:

1. **A priced allowance** in the estimate,
2. **A clarification** sent to the client, or
3. **A clear assumption** stated in the offer.

> Nothing is left to "we'll see during execution."
