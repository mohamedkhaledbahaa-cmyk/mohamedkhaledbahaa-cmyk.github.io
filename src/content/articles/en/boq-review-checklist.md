The Bill of Quantities is the foundation of every bid. Any mistake in it turns into a direct loss after award, so reviewing it before pricing isn't optional.

## Why review matters

In most electrical tenders, the BOQ is prepared quickly and often has missing items or quantities that don't match the drawings. The contractor who spots this early wins twice: a more accurate price, and strong clarifications that show expertise.

## The checklist

### 1. Cross-check the documents

1. **Compare quantities against drawings** — especially cable lengths, lighting points and outlets.
2. **Check the specifications** — does each item match the spec (insulation type, cross-section, IP rating)?
3. **Confirm units of measure** — metres, numbers, lump sums. Every "Lot" item needs an internal breakdown.

### 2. Missing items

4. **Related civil works** — excavation, backfill, panel plinths and sleeves.
5. **Earthing and lightning protection** — often forgotten or given a provisional quantity.
6. **Testing & Commissioning** — and calibration certificates.
7. **Shop drawings and as-built drawings.**

### 3. Commercial risks

8. **Provisional sums** — understand how they'll be measured.
9. **Vague or open items** — add them to your clarification list.
10. **Price escalation** — does the contract allow adjustment for copper and steel?

> Golden rule: never price an item you don't fully understand — clarify first.

| Item | Note | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Cable 4×240 mm² | Length 15% below drawings | Clarify |
| Main distribution board | Breaker spec not defined | Clarify |
| Earthing grid | Missing from BOQ | Qualify in bid |
