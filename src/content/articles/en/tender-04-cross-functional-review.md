**A strong offer is never the work of one department.**

A common pattern: the tendering team prices alone and sends the bid to other departments "for a quick look" two days before submission. Comments arrive late, there is no time to act on them, and the risks they flag get signed into the contract.

## Parallel review

Once tender documents arrive, they go to **every discipline at the same time**:

- **Tendering:** technical review, quantities, RFQs, cost estimate, technical proposal.
- **Contracts:** conditions, obligations and contractual risks.
- **Planning:** preliminary schedule, milestones, manpower histogram.
- **Quality & HSE:** quality plan, HSE plan, risk assessment.
- **Finance:** bonds, guarantees, bank documents.

Working in parallel gives each discipline its full review time — and lets findings feed each other. Planning finds the duration is too short; Contracts finds a harsh LD clause; Tendering prices the consequence.

## One voice to the client

All comments are **consolidated into one RFI list** and sent through **a single official channel**. Answers and addenda are logged and fed back into the offer; anything unanswered becomes a stated assumption.

> One voice to the client, one version of the truth internally.
