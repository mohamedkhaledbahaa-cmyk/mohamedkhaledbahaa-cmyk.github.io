Saying **"No-Go" is sometimes the best commercial decision** a contractor can make.

Estimating capacity is limited. Every tender you price consumes engineering hours, supplier goodwill and management attention. Spread that capacity across every invitation you receive and the quality of each bid drops.

## Four questions before you commit

Before committing estimating hours to a tender, ask:

- **Does the scope fit our capabilities and capacity?** Technically, and in terms of the people and equipment we can actually mobilise.
- **What bonds and financial exposure are required?** Bid bond, performance bond, advance-payment guarantee, retention and payment terms.
- **What are the site's HSE and environmental risks?** Complex methods, hazardous works, environmental restrictions.
- **Which difficulties can we already anticipate — and can we mitigate them?**

## Make it a documented decision

The answers go into a short report for top management, and the decision — Go or No-Go — is **recorded either way**. A documented "No" is valuable: when the same client or project type comes back, you know exactly why you declined last time, and you can measure later whether your bid decisions were right.

> A documented, management-level Go/No-Go decision keeps the pipeline focused on opportunities we can win and deliver well. **Every hour spent on the wrong tender is an hour lost on the right one.**
