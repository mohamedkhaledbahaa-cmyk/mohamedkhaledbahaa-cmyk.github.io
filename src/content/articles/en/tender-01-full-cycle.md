A winning tender doesn't start at pricing. **It starts with deciding whether to bid at all.**

Many contractors treat tendering as a pricing exercise: receive the documents, price them as fast as possible, submit. The result is a busy team, a low hit rate and — worst of all — projects won at a price nobody fully understood.

## The seven stages

A structured tendering process covers the full cycle:

1. **Pre-tender evaluation** — scope, size, bonds, HSE and environmental risks.
2. **A documented Go/No-Go decision** by top management.
3. **Anticipated difficulties** and a mitigation plan from day one.
4. **Parallel input** from Tendering, Contracts, Planning, Quality, HSE and Finance.
5. **Consolidated RFIs** and controlled client correspondence.
6. **Win/loss analysis** after every result.
7. **A complete handover package** to the execution team.

## Why structure pays off

Each stage removes a different kind of surprise:

- The Go/No-Go stage keeps the team focused on bids it can win and deliver.
- The difficulties plan turns risks into priced items, clarifications or stated assumptions.
- Parallel review means contractual, planning and HSE issues surface while there is still time to price them.
- Win/loss analysis and handover make sure the knowledge built during the tender is not lost.

> **The goal: fewer surprises after award, and better decisions before it.**
